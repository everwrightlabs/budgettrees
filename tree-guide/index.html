Tree guide

Know your trees.

The six trees we work on most in the Conejo Valley, and what each one needs. Not sure what you’ve got? Text us a photo.

  • No. 01

    Coast live oak

    Quercus agrifolia

    The tree the valley is named for.

    • Light, careful pruning only, ideally in July or August. Heavy cuts stress an old oak for years.
    • Keep sprinklers and piled soil away from the trunk.

    Permit rules: Protected in every city here and in Oak Park. Removing one, or cutting more than small branches, needs a permit (in Calabasas, even deadwood does).

  • No. 02

    California sycamore

    Platanus racemosa

    White bark, huge limbs, fast growth.

    • Wet springs bring leaf blight (anthracnose). Thinning lets air through the crown.
    • Big limbs over roofs and driveways deserve a look every few years.

    Permit rules: Protected in Agoura Hills (6 inches across) and Oak Park. In Thousand Oaks, a landmark tree from 12 inches, except on home lots under 2 acres.

  • No. 03

    Mexican fan palm

    Washingtonia robusta

    Tall, fast and messy.

    • Dead fronds hang on as a skirt: fire fuel and a home for rats.
    • We trim, pull the seed pods and clean up old trunks.
  • No. 04

    California pepper tree

    Schinus molle

    Graceful, weeping, and heavier than it looks.

    • Long limbs load up with weight and can split in wind.
    • Regular thinning takes the weight off before it breaks.
  • No. 05

    Eucalyptus

    Eucalyptus spp.

    Tall, fast and brittle.

    • Known for dropping big limbs on hot, still summer days.
    • Keep heavy limbs off roofs, driveways and play areas.
  • No. 06

    Fruit & citrus

    Citrus, stone fruit & more

    Backyard trees that pay you back.

    • Prune most deciduous fruit trees in winter, while they’re dormant. Apricots and cherries are the exception: July or August.
    • Citrus only needs a light trim after harvest.

Oak rules differ by city (and by county, in Oak Park), and some permits need an arborist’s report. We’ll tell you what applies to your tree before any work starts, or see the rules for trimming and removal.
